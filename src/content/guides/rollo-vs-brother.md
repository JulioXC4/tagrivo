Both printers can belong on a shipping desk, but the decision is more useful when framed around workflow than a single “best” label. This is a specification-based comparison, not a hands-on review.

## The core difference

[Rollo Wireless](/printers/rollo-wireless/) uses 203 dpi direct-thermal printing with Wi-Fi and a USB fallback. [Brother QL-1110NWB](/printers/brother-ql1110nwb/) offers 300 dpi, a cutter, and a broader set of interfaces including Ethernet.

Rollo accepts generic direct-thermal stock. Brother uses DK-format rolls. These differences affect stock purchasing, loading, and available workspace.

## When Rollo is worth examining

If your main concern is printing from a computer and phone over a shared Wi-Fi network, examine the Rollo setup instructions first. Its AirPrint certification is relevant to compatible Apple workflows.

Do not mistake Bluetooth-assisted setup for direct Bluetooth printing. Also leave room for externally fed media behind the printer. The standard Android mobile flow has a documented 4 × 6 restriction.

## When Brother is worth examining

Brother's 300 dpi specification may matter when you want finer printed detail. The automatic cutter and Ethernet connection are concrete differences that may suit a particular workstation.

They also introduce different considerations: price compatible DK rolls by usable label, verify the current OS support matrix, and check whether the listing is the NWB or another variant. The manufacturer's address-label speed claim is not a claim about full shipping-label throughput.

## What this comparison cannot establish

We have not measured scan reliability, setup time, long-term failure rates, or customer support responsiveness. Resolution alone cannot establish any of those outcomes.

We also do not have verified live prices for both products. Brother's official page listed $334.99 when checked October 28, 2026; this is a dated reference, not an offer. Get current quotes before comparing ownership cost.
