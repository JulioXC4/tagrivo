The cheapest box of “thermal labels” is not automatically the right consumable. First match the printing technology, then the exact printer’s loading and sensing requirements, and finally the shipping document size. Price per pack becomes useful only after those checks.

**Rollo and the specified MUNBYN shipping models document generic direct-thermal stock. Brother QL models use compatible DK-format rolls. Phomemo PM241-BT requires an exact media check.** This is not a universal accessory recommendation.

## Direct thermal is different from thermal transfer

The seven printers in this catalog use direct-thermal media: heat activates the label surface, without a separate ink ribbon. A thermal-transfer system uses a ribbon with compatible stock. A search result for “thermal transfer labels” is not evidence that they will work in a direct-thermal desktop printer.

Even among direct-thermal materials, adhesive, coating, liner, sensing marks and physical loading can differ. Confirm storage and handling guidance if the parcel will face heat, sunlight, moisture or long storage. We do not claim independently measured durability.

## 4 × 6 inches is not exactly 100 × 150 mm

A true 4 × 6 inch label is 101.6 × 152.4 mm. Some stock is sold as 100 × 150 mm. The difference can matter when a document is printed at 100% with content close to the edge. Check stock dimensions, source PDF dimensions, driver paper size and the model’s actual printable width separately.

A maximum print width is not a maximum roll diameter. Nor does the stock’s liner width establish that every pixel can be printed to its edge. Use the [label preview](/tools/label-preview/) to understand clipping, then verify the real print dialog and official media specification.

## Fanfold and rolls need different desk space

Rollo and the selected MUNBYN shipping models document externally fed stock. Fanfold labels can rest behind the printer; a roll may need a suitable holder with the correct direction and unobstructed feed. Confirm alignment, supported media dimensions and sensing before ordering in bulk.

We do not list a universal roll holder: clearance, roll diameter, spindle requirements and pull resistance need to match the printer and stock. An accessory pictured alongside a printer is not proof it is supplied or compatible.

## Brother DK: match the actual part number

QL-1100 and QL-1110NWB use specified DK die-cut or continuous rolls with an automatic cutter. Loose generic 4 × 6 fanfold is not a substitute for that loading system. A third-party product saying “DK-compatible” still needs the exact supported format and holder; it is not automatically a genuine Brother consumable.

The US Brother brochure lists DK-1247 shipping stock at 4.07 × 6.4 inches and DK-2205 continuous paper at 2.4 inches wide. Those dimensions serve different jobs. Match the provider’s document and supported driver format rather than calling both a standard 4 × 6 label. Regional DK part numbers can also differ.

See the [labels and accessories reference](/labels-accessories/) for documented families and the source brochure, without guessed Amazon accessory links.

## Phomemo: resolve the width before buying specialty stock

The PM241-BT page confirms a 4 × 6 workflow but contains inconsistent width ranges. It also warns against HUOJI labels. For another size or brand, confirm current requirements rather than extending a generic-media claim from a Rollo or MUNBYN model.

## Compare cost per usable label

Divide your current stock quote by the usable label count, then allow for waste. Continuous-roll length alone does not tell you how many shipping labels a workflow will cut after spacing and setup. The [cost calculator](/cost-calculator/) accepts your own assumptions and explains its exclusions.

If labels print too small, investigate the [document and scale checklist](/guides/etsy-4x6-label-size/) before blaming stock or purchasing replacement hardware.
